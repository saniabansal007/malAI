# Standard Operating Procedure: Consumer Privacy & Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)
**Document ID:** POL-PRIV-2026-V2  
**Regulatory References:** Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. 1681), Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA / Regulation P - 12 CFR Part 1016)

---

### Section 301: Permissible Purpose for Credit Reports (FCRA)
301.1 Permissible Purpose Mandate:
Under 15 U.S.C. 1681b, the bank may obtain and use a consumer credit report ONLY when a documented permissible purpose exists, strictly limited to:
- In accordance with the written instructions of the consumer to whom it relates;
- In connection with a credit transaction involving the consumer on which the consumer has applied or for review or collection of an account;
- For employment purposes (only with clear, standalone written authorization from the consumer);
- In connection with the determination of the consumer's eligibility for a license or benefit granted by a governmental instrumentality.

301.2 Prohibition of Unauthorized "Curiosity" Inquiries:
Employees are strictly prohibited from accessing credit reports, account histories, or non-public personal information (NPI) of family members, acquaintances, celebrities, or colleagues without a legitimate, open business application. Unauthorized access constitutes a federal misdemeanor and grounds for immediate termination.

---

### Section 302: Consumer Disputes & Furnisher Obligations
302.1 Investigation Timelines:
Upon receiving notice of a consumer dispute from a Consumer Reporting Agency (CRA) or directly from the consumer regarding reported credit information, the bank must conduct an investigation and complete all required reporting within thirty (30) calendar days of receipt.

302.2 Correction of Inaccurate Data:
If the investigation determines that the information was inaccurate, incomplete, or cannot be verified, the bank must immediately modify, delete, or permanently block the reporting of that information across all CRAs to which the bank furnishes data (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion).

---

### Section 303: Privacy Disclosures & Non-Public Personal Information (GLBA)
303.1 Annual and Initial Privacy Notices:
The bank must provide a clear and conspicuous notice that accurately reflects its privacy policies and practices to every customer not later than when establishing the customer relationship, and at least once in each 12-consecutive-month period thereafter.

303.2 Consumer Opt-Out for Third-Party Sharing:
Customers must be given a reasonable opportunity and simple means to opt out of the disclosure of Non-Public Personal Information (NPI) to nonaffiliated third parties before any such information is transferred, except under statutory exceptions (e.g., fraud prevention, subpoena compliance, or joint marketing agreements).
